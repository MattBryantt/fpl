The four sources

1. The official FPL API

https://fantasy.premierleague.com/api/bootstrap-static/ and /fixtures/. Public, unauthenticated, undocumented but stable for years. This is the authority for anything the game itself defines: who exists, what position they are, what they cost, who they play for, the fixture list, gameweek deadlines, and the chip windows.

It also carries Opta-derived underlying stats — expected_goals, expected_assists, expected_goals_conceded, and since 2025/26 the defensive-contribution counts (clearances_blocks_interceptions, recoveries, tackles, defensive_contribution). So "Opta data" is already in here; there is no separate Opta feed to buy.

Important quirk: before gameweek 1, the API serves last completed season's totals on every player. That is what this whole model is built on preseason. Meanwhile strength_attack_home and the other team-strength fields are zeroed out, which is why team strength is derived from xG and odds instead of taken from the API.

2. Understat

Non-penalty xG, xA, xGChain and xGBuildup per player. Understat stopped embedding its datasets in the league page HTML — that page is now a JavaScript shell with no data in it — but the AJAX endpoint the page calls is still open: 

POST https://understat.com/main/getPlayersStats/
     league=EPL&season=2025
     X-Requested-With: XMLHttpRequest

The key thing it gives that the FPL API does not is non-penalty xG. Penalties are worth modelling separately and assigning to the designated taker, rather than smearing them across everyone with a high xG total, and you cannot do that from a combined number.

A player who moved mid-season appears with a comma-joined club, "Bournemouth,Manchester City". Those rows are excluded from team aggregates — a transferred player's xG cannot be split between his two clubs, and assigning it all to either badly distorts that club's rate — but he is still indexed under both clubs for the player join.

3. Per-gameweek history archive (third-party, opt-in)

Weighting recent form above early-season form needs match-by-match data, and neither live source can provide it. The FPL API wipes element-summary history when a season rolls over and keeps only season totals in history_past; Understat's endpoint returns whole-season aggregates and silently ignores date parameters — I tested three spellings and all returned byte-identical payloads.

So recency weighting reads from the vaastav/Fantasy-Premier-League archive, a long-running community mirror that snapshots the FPL API every gameweek and publishes CSV. It carries all 38 gameweeks with xG, xA, xGC, defensive contribution, saves and bonus.

This one is not official. It is a third party that can lag, change shape or disappear, which is why recency weighting is off by default and why a failure to fetch costs you the tilt and nothing else — the projection carries on with flat season rates. Element ids are reassigned every season, so rows are joined to current players through code, the stable per-player identifier that survives both season rollovers and transfers.

4. The Odds API (bookmakers)

https://api.the-odds-api.com/v4/sports/soccer_epl/odds, markets h2h and totals. Free tier, 500 requests a month, key in .env. Around twenty books are returned per fixture; the median price per outcome is taken so one outlier cannot move the line.

Prices carry an overround — the implied probabilities sum to more than 1, and the excess is the bookmaker's margin. That is removed by normalising, then the de-vigged 1×2 probabilities plus the over/under line are inverted into expected goals for each side via a Poisson model.

This source is optional. Without a key everything still runs on xG-derived team ratings; the odds are simply the sharpest forward-looking estimate available, and the books only price about a fortnight ahead, so the further gameweeks fall back to ratings regardless.

Joining them together

The three sources agree on nothing. FPL says Gabriel Fernando de Jesus, Understat says Gabriel Jesus; the books say Nottingham Forest where FPL says Nott'm Forest. Reconciling them is fplkit/matching.py's entire job, and it runs in stages, most precise first, with every stage sweeping all players before the next begins.

StageScopeRule
1. Exactwithin clubnormalised full name or web name matches exactly
2. Structuralwithin clubfirst name agrees and at least one further name is shared
3. Exactwhole leaguecatches summer movers, filed under a club they have left
4. Structuralwhole leaguesame rule, no club constraint
5. Fuzzywithin clubtoken-set similarity, last resort, only unclaimed rows

Order matters because an Understat row can only be claimed once. Three Arsenal players are called Gabriel: the centre-back must take the row named plainly "Gabriel" in stage 1 before Martinelli and Jesus are allowed to compete for it in stage 2. Running each player to exhaustion instead would let whoever came first in the table take it — and quietly hand a winger a centre-back's expected goals.

Why fuzzy matching is last. token_set_ratio returns a perfect 100 whenever one name's tokens are a subset of the other's. It scores "Will Dennis" against "Dennis Cirkin" as a certainty, and "Gabriel" against "Gabriel Martinelli Silva" as confidently as it scores the real Gabriel. It only ever sees rows nobody else wanted. When matching fails it fails closed: the player keeps FPL-only rates, which is what he would have had anyway.

From data to points

1. Fixture → expected goals. De-vigged 1×2 + over/under, inverted to (λhome, λaway) by least squares under independent Poisson. Unpriced fixtures use xG-derived attack and defence ratings applied on top of a fixed league-average goals rate.
2. Team → player. The player's non-penalty xG and xA per 90 are scaled by this fixture's expected goals relative to his team's season average, and by his expected minutes. Penalties go to the designated taker only.
3. Player → points. The FPL scoring rules, with the non-linear terms — clean sheets, the −1 per two conceded, saves, defensive contribution — evaluated over their full Poisson distributions and across the start and substitute cases separately.

Caching and refresh

Every response is cached to .cache/ on disk with a TTL: FPL for 3 hours, odds for 6, Understat for 24. This is not just politeness — the odds free tier is 500 requests a month, and an uncached UI would burn it in an afternoon. python fpl.py cache-clear empties it; --refresh on any command bypasses it for that run.

What none of it can tell you

  • Minutes. Everything above is a rounding error next to whether a player starts, and last season's starts cannot see a transfer, a new manager or a changed pecking order. This is the weakest input in the model, and the reason the stat editor exists.
  • Form within a season. Every rate is a season average. A player mid-hot-streak looks identical to one who front-loaded his returns.
  • Anything from preseason friendlies. No source here covers them.
  • Team news. Only what the FPL API's status and news fields carry, which lag press conferences.
  • Double and blank gameweeks beyond what is already on the fixture list — they appear when cup rounds are drawn and games get postponed, which is why chip advice stays silent early in the season.
  • Set-piece duties beyond penalties, so a new corner taker is undervalued.

Independent Poisson also understates draws by roughly two points of probability, which slightly overstates decisive results and therefore clean sheets. Dixon-Coles would correct it; the effect is small next to the minutes uncertainty above.
